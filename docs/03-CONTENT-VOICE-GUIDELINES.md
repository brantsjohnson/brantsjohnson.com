# 03. Content & Voice Guidelines

**Plain English purpose:** This doc governs every word that appears on the public site. It exists so the amount of text stays minimal, nothing repeats itself, and the site reads like it was written once, carefully, by one person, rather than generated in bulk.

---

## 1. The absolute rules

1. **No dashes, anywhere, ever, in on-site copy.** No em dash, no en dash, and no hyphen used as punctuation (joining clauses). This includes headings, buttons, captions, chatbot responses rendered in the UI, and error messages. Compound words that genuinely require a hyphen (like "up-to-date") are fine, that's spelling, not punctuation. If you catch yourself writing a dash to join two thoughts, split it into two sentences instead, or use a comma or period.
   - Wrong: "The project, still in progress, will launch soon."  *(comma is fine, this is just an example of restructuring)*
   - Wrong: "The project is still in progress, it will launch soon."
   - Wrong (dash): "The project is still in progress; it will launch soon." (semicolon is acceptable if truly needed, but prefer two sentences)
   - Right: "The project is still in progress. It launches soon."
2. **No redundancy.** Any given fact, label, or call to action appears exactly once, in the one place it belongs, at the size that matches its importance. It does not reappear later, smaller, "just in case." If two sections both want to reference something (e.g. "Contact"), one of them links to the other; the information itself is not duplicated.
3. **Minimal words, maximum clarity.** Default to the shortest correct sentence. If a sentence can lose a word without losing meaning, cut it.
4. **No filler, no AI-sounding phrasing.** Avoid throat-clearing ("In today's world...", "As we all know..."), avoid stacked adjectives, avoid corporate-speak ("leverage," "utilize," "seamless synergy"). Write the way you'd say it out loud to one specific person.

---

## 2. Voice

- First person where it's genuinely you speaking (About, project descriptions).
- Confident, plainspoken, specific. Prefer concrete detail ("built the recommendation engine that ran X") over vague claims ("passionate about innovation").
- Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
- Numbers, dates, and specifics build more credibility than adjectives. Prefer a real number over a superlative.

---

## 3. UI copy specifics

- **Buttons:** verb first, 1 to 3 words. "View project," not "Click here to view this project."
- **Empty states:** brief, human, no dead air. "Nothing here yet" beats a blank box.
- **Errors:** say what happened and what to do, nothing else. "That email looks off. Try again."
- **Labels appear once.** A form field is labeled once. It does not also get a placeholder repeating the label.

---

## 4. User-provided content (uploaded documents, videos, links)

When you (the site owner) upload a document, a video link, or similar source material through the admin portal or feed it to Cursor to place on the site: **that content is not rewritten by AI.** It gets formatted (typography, spacing, embedding) to match the design system, but the words themselves are yours, untouched. This is different from copy Cursor or Grok Bot might draft for UI labels, meta descriptions, or social captions, where AI assistance is expected but must still follow every rule in this document.

---

## 5. Checklist before publishing any copy

- [ ] No dashes used as punctuation anywhere in this text.
- [ ] Nothing here is said elsewhere on the site in a smaller/quieter way.
- [ ] Every sentence earns its place; nothing is filler.
- [ ] Reads like a specific person wrote it, not a template.
- [ ] If this is user-uploaded source material, it was formatted, not rewritten.
