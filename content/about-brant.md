## Who I am

Brant S. Johnson is a senior product manager, a creator, performer and entrepreneur, and an activist for the places that raised him. He lives in American Fork, Utah, and he is open to relocating. The public line is: Good things deserve to happen. He builds technology as a bridge to real human connection.

He has worked in product management, performing, media, event production, and government. He gets very invested in other people's ideas and likes helping them get built. He is especially drawn to events and software that help people connect.

## What he is building

Intro: Founder and Product Lead, November 2025 to present. AI software for event organizers. It recommends introductions for attendees, sponsors, and organizers and explains each recommendation in plain language. Success means accepted recommendations and completed meetings, not clicks. Intro came out of his work on Utah Business events and sponsor conversations. Live at https://intro.events. Intro's CRM features are still in progress and have not shipped, so do not describe them as live.

Bridger: Founder, April 2025 to present. Social software that helps people become friends around what they love. He also wants it to help third places, such as community spaces and local gathering spots, bring people together more often. That third-place idea is his vision, not a claim that community deployments are live. It is in a TestFlight beta, built with contracted engineers and designers. No public user count. Live at https://bridger.social

## Crew Finance

Senior Product Manager and first employee, December 2022 to April 2024, remote. He was the only product manager. He helped build Crew's debit-card program from the ground up, coordinating issuing, processing, KYC, card lifecycle, fulfillment, and family onboarding. Crew's debit card was issued by Bangor Savings Bank on Mastercard. He owned the Mastercard card issuing program, set up KYC with banking and compliance partners, and cut onboarding time in half. PCI and COPPA requirements were part of the program. He ran manufacturing work for the physical cards and their packaging, and kept prototypes separate from what shipped. He built the financial models for a raise. At a startup he wore many hats. When outside designers could not land the brand, he taught himself to draw on an iPad and drew the Crew brand himself, including the 2024 wordmark. Crew still uses that wordmark. He worked with the UX designer to carry the identity across the app, website, card, and packaging.

## Service and leadership

Uintah Resilience Archway in Vernal, Utah: at eighteen he designed a replacement for the old arch over his high school's road and called companies for money, steel, engineering help, and time. He did not do the welding. It took about five years, three principals, two school boards, and two superintendents. The arch is about 25 feet tall and 63 feet wide, modular, and raised five feet so buses can pass under it. Seniors take graduation photos there. The build ran from 2017 to 2023, and leftover funds helped start the Resilience Scholarship.

Resilience Scholarship: he founded it in 2020 for graduating seniors, and he started a weekly meeting with school counselors.

Habitat for Humanity, Hilo, Hawaii: trip leader, May to August 2021. He led 22 youth over a series of 10 days to build a home.

TEDxSaltLakeCity: Organization Assistant, January to September 2021. He directed monthly meetings, set agendas and minutes, and designed brand creative.
